# Narrative for "Responsive Teaching in Action"

## Core Thesis

Responsive teaching means designing lessons with evidence-based decision points.

This deck is compact and practical. It takes the big idea of formative assessment and turns it into a classroom sequence: clarify the destination, elicit evidence from all students, interpret the evidence, give feedback that causes thinking, and adapt what happens next.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The deck begins with the central reason for formative assessment: students do not learn exactly what teachers teach. Because learning depends on prior knowledge and because performance today may not last, teachers need to build plan B into plan A. The deck then introduces the five formative assessment strategies, focusing especially on eliciting evidence and feedback. It shows how diagnostic and hinge questions can reveal student thinking quickly, then closes with principles for feedback that moves learning forward.

## The Four Acts of the Deck

### Act 1: Teaching Requires Contingency

Relevant slides: 1-6

Key message:

> A good lesson is planned, but not rigid.

Narration:

"The opening argument is that students do not always learn what we teach. So responsive teaching means planning for decision points. We do not just plan the explanation and activity. We plan how we will find out what students understood and what we will do with that evidence."

Use the memorable phrase:

> "Build Plan B into Plan A."

### Act 2: Elicit Evidence From All Students

Relevant slides: 7-19

Key message:

> The teacher needs evidence from the whole room, not volunteers.

Narration:

"Questioning has two purposes: to cause thinking and to collect evidence. If only confident students respond, the teacher is not seeing the class. Wiliam's practical alternatives include no hands up, all-student response systems, ABCD cards, mini-whiteboards, and hinge questions."

The crucial design principle:

> A correct response should indicate correct thinking.

That is why distractor-driven questions matter. The wrong answers should reveal likely misconceptions.

### Act 3: Use Hinge Questions To Decide What Happens Next

Relevant slides: 10-18

Key message:

> A hinge question is not a quiz item. It is a teaching decision tool.

Narration:

"A hinge question sits at a point in the lesson where the teacher needs to know whether to move on, reteach, give more practice, or change the task. Every student answers quickly, and the teacher can interpret responses quickly."

Practical criteria:

- based on a critical concept;
- answerable by every student within two minutes;
- interpretable by the teacher in about 30 seconds;
- includes distractors based on real misconceptions;
- leads to a planned instructional response.

### Act 4: Feedback Should Make Students Think

Relevant slides: 20-29

Key message:

> Feedback is useful only when it changes what students do next.

Narration:

"The feedback section warns that feedback is not automatically beneficial. Grades can stop students reading comments. Comments can be too directive. Feedback can lower achievement if it shifts attention away from the task or convinces students the goal is out of reach."

Use the general principles:

- improve the student, not just the work;
- focus on action;
- cause thinking;
- give students time to respond;
- make feedback more work for the learner than the teacher.

## Suggested Opening Script

"Responsive teaching is the daily version of formative assessment. It asks: what do students understand right now, and what should I do next? The key is to design lessons with moments where evidence changes the path of instruction."

## Suggested Closing Script

"The simplest version of responsive teaching is a loop: plan, elicit, interpret, respond. If evidence does not change anything, it is not formative. If feedback does not lead to student action, it is not useful."

## Workshop Activity

Teachers choose one upcoming lesson and identify:

1. The hinge point where they need evidence.
2. The misconception they most need to detect.
3. A multiple-choice or all-student response question.
4. The action they will take if most students are secure.
5. The action they will take if many students are not secure.
