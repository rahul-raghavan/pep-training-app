# Narrative for "Formative Assessment Full Course"

## Core Thesis

This deck is the full architecture of Wiliam's formative assessment model.

It is not a single workshop deck. It is a multi-session course covering the definition of formative assessment, eliciting evidence, feedback, the learner's role, and teacher learning communities. Use it as the reference deck for building the full manual and LMS sequence.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The course begins by distinguishing formative assessment from summative evaluation and assessment for learning. The central definition is that assessment becomes formative when evidence is elicited, interpreted, and used to make better decisions about future instruction. Wiliam then justifies formative assessment from five perspectives: cross-cultural, intuitive, functional, equity, and empirical. The course then moves through the five key strategies: clarify learning intentions, elicit evidence, provide feedback, activate peers, and activate students as owners of learning. The final session shifts from classroom practice to implementation: teacher learning communities are needed because teacher change requires choice, flexibility, small steps, accountability, and support.

## The Course Arc

### Session 1: What Formative Assessment Is and Is Not

Relevant slides: 1-60

Key message:

> Formative assessment is defined by use, not by format.

Narration:

"A test is not formative because it is called formative. An exit ticket is not formative because it is short. Evidence becomes formative only when it is used by teachers, students, or peers to make better decisions than they would otherwise have made."

Use the core definition from slide 10:

> Evidence is elicited, interpreted, and used to make better decisions about future instruction.

Key distinctions:

- formative vs summative depends on inference and use;
- assessment for learning is broader than formative assessment;
- retrieval practice can improve learning even when it is not formative;
- formative assessment is about decisions, not labels.

### Session 2: Eliciting Evidence

Relevant slides: 61-118

Key message:

> Good questions reveal thinking from all students.

Narration:

"The course then turns to evidence. The teacher cannot respond to learning that remains invisible. Questioning should cause thinking and provide data for instruction. The deck emphasizes no hands up, random selection, no opting out, all-student responses, diagnostic questions, and hinge questions."

Practical design rule:

> The wrong answers should be useful.

Distractors should represent real misconceptions, not random errors.

### Session 3: Feedback That Moves Learning Forward

Relevant slides: 119-198

Key message:

> Feedback is only useful if students use it to improve.

Narration:

"The feedback session is a major part of the course because feedback has high potential and high risk. Feedback can raise achievement, but a large proportion of feedback interventions have negative effects. The deck moves from feedback as information to feedback as learner action."

Important principles:

- grades can block the effect of comments;
- feedback should cause thinking;
- feedback should focus on the task and process, not ego;
- wise feedback combines high standards with belief in the learner;
- students need time and routines to act on feedback.

### Session 4: The Learner's Role

Relevant slides: 199-254

Key message:

> Students must become resources for one another and owners of their own learning.

Narration:

"Formative assessment is not just something teachers do to students. Students need to understand quality, compare work, support peers, self-assess, and regulate their own learning. Peer assessment is not mainly about students grading each other. It is a bridge to self-assessment."

Key practices:

- examples before rubrics;
- anonymous peer assessment before self-assessment;
- pair and group routines;
- practice testing;
- self-assessment of understanding;
- student-generated questions.

### Session 5: Making Teacher Change Happen

Relevant slides: 255-318

Key message:

> Teacher learning requires structures, not just information.

Narration:

"The final section is the implementation engine. Wiliam argues that teachers need evidence, ideas, choice, flexibility, small steps, accountability, and support. Teacher learning communities provide the structure for sustained experimentation and peer accountability."

Core implementation idea:

> Teacher professional development should be a repeated cycle of trying a specific technique, returning with evidence, getting feedback, and planning the next step.

## Suggested Opening Script

"This course is the spine of the program. It shows that formative assessment is not a toolkit of tricks. It is a way of making classroom decisions using evidence. The same five questions come back throughout: where are students going, where are they now, how do we close the gap, who can help, and how do students take more ownership?"

## Suggested Closing Script

"If we take this course seriously, the school changes in two ways. Classrooms become more responsive because teachers collect and use better evidence. Professional development becomes more effective because teachers work on specific techniques over time, with colleagues, evidence, and follow-up."

## LMS Assessment Ideas

Use scenario questions:

1. Is this assessment being used formatively or summatively?
2. Which hinge question best diagnoses the misconception?
3. Which feedback comment is most likely to cause thinking?
4. Which peer-assessment routine is most likely to build self-assessment?
5. Which TLC structure is most likely to change practice over time?

The final message:

> Formative assessment is the classroom habit of making better decisions with evidence.
