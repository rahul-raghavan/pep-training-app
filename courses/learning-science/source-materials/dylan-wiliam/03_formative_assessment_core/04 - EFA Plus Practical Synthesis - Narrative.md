# Narrative for "EFA Plus Practical Synthesis"

## Core Thesis

This deck is a recent synthesis of formative assessment plus self-regulated learning, motivation, and implementation.

It is useful because it does not treat EFA as a narrow assessment initiative. It frames formative assessment as part of a bigger goal: raising achievement, supporting equity, developing resilient learners, and helping teachers implement practical routines over time.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The deck opens with the social and economic case for raising achievement, then defines formative assessment and distinguishes it from assessment for learning. Wiliam develops four perspectives: intuitive, functional, equity, and empirical. The deck then moves into strategies and techniques: learning intentions, eliciting evidence, feedback, peer learning, and student ownership. The later sections broaden into self-regulated learning, metacognition, motivation, beliefs, and teacher learning communities. It is best read as an integrated capstone rather than a first introduction.

## The Five Acts of the Deck

### Act 1: Achievement Matters, But Not at Any Cost

Relevant slides: 7-21

Key message:

> Schools need higher achievement, a broad curriculum, and students who want to keep learning.

Narration:

"The deck starts by asking why achievement matters: not just for test scores, but for life chances, health, work, civic participation, and agency. But it also warns that we cannot raise achievement by narrowing the curriculum or making school joyless."

Use this as motivation for leaders:

> We need improvement strategies that raise achievement while preserving rich education.

### Act 2: Formative Assessment Is a Response to Uncertainty

Relevant slides: 22-60

Key message:

> Teachers need evidence because students do not simply learn what was taught.

Narration:

"The intuitive argument is the same central Wiliam idea: prior knowledge matters, students do not learn exactly what we teach, and teaching needs plan B built into plan A. Formative assessment is the process for making those adjustments."

Clarify the meaning:

- not a test type;
- not a synonym for assessment for learning;
- not data collection for its own sake;
- a decision process involving evidence, interpretation, and action.

### Act 3: The Five Strategies Become Classroom Techniques

Relevant slides: 60-160

Key message:

> The framework matters only if it becomes routines teachers can actually use.

Narration:

"The middle of the deck turns the five strategies into practical techniques: clarifying learning intentions, eliciting evidence with diagnostic questions, giving feedback that causes thinking, activating peers, and developing students as owners of learning."

Key extraction targets:

- examples of learning intentions and success criteria;
- hinge-question design;
- distractor-driven questions;
- feedback as detective work;
- peer and self-assessment routines;
- techniques teachers can choose from and trial.

### Act 4: Student Ownership Requires Metacognition and Motivation

Relevant slides: 160-273

Key message:

> Student ownership is not a slogan; it requires teachable routines.

Narration:

"Wiliam connects formative assessment to self-regulated learning. Students need to plan, monitor, evaluate, retrieve, revise, and attribute success and failure to controllable causes. Motivation is treated carefully: it is not a substance teachers pour into students. Achievement, competence, challenge, feedback, and beliefs interact."

Practical implications:

- teach metacognitive questions inside subject work;
- model planning, monitoring, and evaluation;
- teach students why retrieval practice works;
- create feedback routines that support agency;
- treat growth mindset as a means to better strategy use, not as a poster.

### Act 5: Implementation Needs Professional Learning Structures

Relevant later slides and recurring TLC sections

Key message:

> EFA changes practice only when teachers work on it together over time.

Narration:

"The deck's practical synthesis only matters if teachers can implement small changes, discuss evidence, and refine practice. The goal is not to persuade teachers once. It is to build a professional learning system."

This connects directly to your program design:

- initial workshops create motivation and shared language;
- LMS modules build knowledge;
- teacher learning communities turn knowledge into classroom habits;
- leaders protect time and focus.

## Suggested Opening Script

"This deck is called EFA+, and the plus matters. Formative assessment is not isolated from learning science, metacognition, motivation, or professional learning. It is the classroom system that connects all of them."

## Suggested Closing Script

"The goal is not for teachers to know the five strategies as a list. The goal is for lessons to become more responsive, students to become more self-regulating, and teacher learning to become more sustained."

## Workshop Activity

Use this deck for a "choose your implementation focus" session. Teachers select one routine:

1. learning intentions and examples;
2. hinge questions;
3. all-student response;
4. feedback as detective work;
5. peer assessment;
6. retrieval practice;
7. metacognitive prompts.

They then plan:

- when they will use it;
- what student evidence they will collect;
- what success would look like;
- what they will bring back to the next meeting.
