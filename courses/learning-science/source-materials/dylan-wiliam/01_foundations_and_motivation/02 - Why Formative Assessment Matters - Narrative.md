# Narrative for "Why Formative Assessment Matters"

## Core Thesis

Formative assessment should be a priority because teaching is uncertain.

Students do not reliably learn what we teach. They bring different prior knowledge, interpret tasks differently, forget quickly, and can perform today without retaining the learning. Formative assessment is the system teachers use to find out what students are actually thinking, then adapt instruction while there is still time to make a difference.

This deck is the best opening argument for why responsive teaching belongs at the center of the professional development program.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The deck begins with a challenge: schools need higher achievement, but cannot get there by narrowing the curriculum or making learning joyless. The solution is not more testing, but better use of evidence during learning. Formative assessment matters because students' prior knowledge is the biggest influence on what they learn next, and teachers cannot assume students learned what was taught. Wiliam then gives four arguments: the intuitive argument, the functional argument, the equity argument, and the empirical argument. Together they show that formative assessment is not a technique or a test; it is a disciplined way of making teaching responsive.

## The Emotional Framing

Present this deck as:

> "We are not adding more assessment. We are improving the quality of decisions during teaching."

Avoid framing it as:

> "Teachers need to collect more data."

The word "assessment" can make teachers think of marking, tests, accountability, and workload. The narrative should keep returning to evidence, decisions, and action.

## The Four Acts of the Deck

### Act 1: The Challenge Is Bigger Than Test Scores

Relevant slides: 1-3

Key message:

> We need better learning without narrowing the curriculum or destroying the desire to keep learning.

Narration:

"The problem is not just that students need higher scores. They need deeper achievement, broader capabilities, and the desire to keep learning. Any improvement strategy that raises test performance by narrowing the curriculum is too small. We need approaches that improve learning inside the normal work of teaching."

This is a useful bridge from teacher quality to learning science: if teachers are the improvement lever, they need practical ways to make better instructional decisions.

### Act 2: The Intuitive Case - Students Do Not Learn What We Teach

Relevant slides: 4-10

Key message:

> If prior knowledge drives learning, teachers need evidence of students' current thinking.

Narration:

"The intuitive argument is simple. What students already know shapes what they can learn next. But students do not simply absorb what we teach. They misunderstand, partially understand, forget, overgeneralize, or appear to understand while only performing in the moment. So teachers need plan B built into plan A."

Slides 5 and 6 are the center of this act.

Use the phrase:

> "Build plan B into plan A."

That is the memorable practical idea. A formative lesson is not a fixed script. It is a lesson designed with decision points.

### Act 3: The Functional Case - Formative Assessment Is a Decision System

Relevant slides: 11-13, 25-41

Key message:

> Formative assessment means using evidence to adapt teaching and learning.

Narration:

"Wiliam's framework asks three questions: where is the learner going, where is the learner now, and how do we get the learner there? These questions can be answered by the teacher, peers, or the learner. That produces the five key strategies: clarify learning intentions, elicit evidence, give feedback, activate peers, and activate students as owners of learning."

This is the framework to reuse across the whole PD program.

The practical sequence is:

1. Clarify the learning destination.
2. Elicit evidence from all students.
3. Interpret the evidence.
4. Decide what to do next.
5. Help students use feedback, peers, and self-assessment to close the gap.

### Act 4: The Equity and Empirical Case - It Is High Impact and Low Cost

Relevant slides: 14-24

Key message:

> Formative assessment is one of the most practical equity strategies because it helps teachers respond before gaps widen.

Narration:

"The equity argument is not just about closing the gap at the end of the year. It is about not allowing gaps to grow unnoticed during lessons and units. If teachers know what students understand now, they can respond now. That is why feedback, peer tutoring, and metacognition appear as cost-effective approaches in the evidence summaries."

The empirical argument matters, but keep it short:

"There is enough evidence to justify serious investment, but the main reason formative assessment matters is that it changes daily classroom decisions."

## Suggested Opening Script

"I want to separate formative assessment from the idea of more tests. Formative assessment is not a test type. It is a way of teaching under uncertainty.

Every lesson contains uncertainty. We do not know exactly what students bring with them. We do not know exactly what they understood from our explanation. We do not know which students are performing fluently but will forget next week. So the question is: how do we make better decisions during learning?

That is the purpose of formative assessment."

## Suggested Closing Script

"The reason formative assessment matters is that teaching is responsive work. If we teach without evidence, we are guessing. If we collect evidence but do nothing with it, it is not formative. The power is in the loop: evidence, interpretation, action.

This is why the rest of the program focuses on learning intentions, questioning, feedback, peer learning, self-regulation, and teacher learning communities. They are all parts of the same system."

## Discussion Questions

1. Where in our current lessons do we already build plan B into plan A?
2. Where are we still waiting too long to find out what students understood?
3. Which evidence-gathering routines would give us information from all students, not just the most confident?
4. How can feedback be redesigned so students have to think and act?
5. What would make formative assessment feel like better teaching rather than extra workload?

## Link to the Training Program

This deck should introduce the whole formative assessment strand:

- Learning intentions: students need to know what quality looks like.
- Eliciting evidence: teachers need to know what students are thinking.
- Feedback: information must lead to learner action.
- Peer learning: students can become resources for each other.
- Self-regulation: students can learn to monitor and improve their own work.

The final message:

> Formative assessment is not more assessment. It is better teaching under conditions of uncertainty.
