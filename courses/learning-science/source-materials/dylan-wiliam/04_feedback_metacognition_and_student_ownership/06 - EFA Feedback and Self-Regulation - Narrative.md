# Narrative for "EFA Feedback and Self-Regulation"

## Core Thesis

This deck is an integrated capstone: formative assessment, feedback, metacognition, motivation, and self-regulated learning all belong to one system.

It is best used after teachers have already encountered the basics of learning science and formative assessment. The through-line is that teachers first make learning visible, then gradually help students use that evidence to regulate their own learning.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The deck begins with several perspectives on formative assessment, including cross-cultural, intuitive, functional, and equity arguments. It then develops the five key strategies and moves into feedback, learning intentions, success criteria, evidence-centered design, metacognition, retrieval practice, motivation, attribution, growth mindset, and social-emotional learning. The final summaries pull the ideas together: formative assessment supports excellence and equity, and self-regulated learning improves achievement when students have metacognitive knowledge, strategies, and productive beliefs.

## The Five Acts of the Deck

### Act 1: Formative Assessment Is Necessary for Equity and Excellence

Relevant slides: 3-28

Key message:

> To improve learning for all students, teachers need evidence during learning.

Narration:

"The opening frames formative assessment from multiple angles. The intuitive argument is that students do not learn what we teach. The equity argument is that feedback, metacognition, and peer learning are high-value ways to close gaps. The functional argument is the five-strategy framework."

### Act 2: Feedback Must Lead to Student Action

Relevant slides: 40-70 and related feedback sections

Key message:

> Feedback is formative only when learners use it to close the gap.

Narration:

"The feedback material emphasizes that feedback is not automatically helpful. It can trigger effort, revision, goal adjustment, rejection, or withdrawal. The teacher's job is to design feedback that causes thinking and keeps students oriented toward growth."

Practical ideas:

- feedback as detective work;
- comment-only or delayed judgement when appropriate;
- time for revision;
- high standards plus belief;
- peer and self-assessment as routes to agency.

### Act 3: Clarity and Evidence Design Make Learning Visible

Relevant slides: 70-120

Key message:

> Students need clear goals, and teachers need evidence that supports valid inferences.

Narration:

"The deck connects learning intentions, success criteria, big ideas, and evidence-centered design. This is more sophisticated than simply checking answers. Teachers need to know what they are trying to infer about student understanding and what evidence would support that inference."

Teacher-facing question:

> "What would students say or do that would convince us they understand?"

### Act 4: Self-Regulated Learning Must Be Taught

Relevant slides: 120-205

Key message:

> Students need explicit support to plan, monitor, evaluate, and retrieve.

Narration:

"The metacognition section shows that self-regulation is not a personality trait. Students need subject-specific metacognitive prompts, strategy instruction, retrieval practice, and feedback on strategy use."

Key routines:

- comprehension, connection, strategy, and reflection questions;
- planning-monitoring-evaluating prompts;
- retrieval practice with feedback;
- strategic resource-use checklists;
- self-assessment with exemplars.

### Act 5: Motivation Is Built Through Competence, Challenge, and Beliefs

Relevant slides: 210-273

Key message:

> Motivation is not a separate add-on; it emerges from learning conditions.

Narration:

"The later slides treat motivation carefully. Achievement can drive later motivation as much as motivation drives achievement. Students' attributions matter: good learners see success and failure as internal, unstable, and specific. Growth mindset matters when it supports strategy choice and challenge-seeking."

Practical conclusion:

> Help students experience progress, use effective strategies, and interpret struggle as information rather than identity.

## Suggested Opening Script

"This deck is about the handoff from teacher regulation to student regulation. At first, the teacher collects evidence and responds. Over time, students learn to use evidence themselves: to plan, monitor, retrieve, revise, and improve."

## Suggested Closing Script

"The goal is a classroom where evidence does not live only with the teacher. Students understand quality, notice gaps, use feedback, help peers, retrieve what they know, and adjust strategies. That is the deeper purpose of formative assessment."

## Workshop Activity

Teachers design a "student ownership loop" for one lesson:

1. Students see examples of quality.
2. Students answer a diagnostic question.
3. Students receive feedback as a prompt.
4. Students revise or retrieve.
5. Students evaluate what changed.

The final message:

> Formative assessment matures when students learn to use evidence for themselves.
