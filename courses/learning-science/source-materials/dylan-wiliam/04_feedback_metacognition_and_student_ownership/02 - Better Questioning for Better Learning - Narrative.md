# Narrative for "Better Questioning for Better Learning"

## Core Thesis

Questioning should make students think and give teachers actionable evidence.

This is a short, practical deck. Its value is that it strips formative assessment down to a concrete classroom move: better questions, answered by more students, used to make better instructional decisions.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The deck starts with Wiliam's formative assessment framework, then focuses on eliciting evidence. The central idea is that questions should both cause thinking and inform teaching. Wiliam uses subject examples to show the difference between ordinary questions and diagnostic questions. The deck ends with hinge questions: questions placed at critical points in lessons so teachers can decide whether to move on, reteach, or adapt.

## The Three Acts of the Deck

### Act 1: Questions Are Evidence Tools

Relevant slides: 1-7

Key message:

> Questions are not just for participation. They are tools for thinking and diagnosis.

Narration:

"Teachers often ask questions to keep students engaged or to check that someone knows the answer. Wiliam's sharper claim is that questions should cause thinking and provide data that informs teaching."

Use slide 7 as the anchor:

- cause thinking;
- provide data;
- generate questions with colleagues;
- focus less on open vs closed and more on what the question reveals.

### Act 2: Diagnostic Questions Reveal Misconceptions

Relevant slides: 8-13

Key message:

> The best wrong answers are informative.

Narration:

"The science, English, and grammar examples show why distractors matter. A good diagnostic question is designed so that each answer option tells the teacher something about how the student is thinking."

Practical rule:

> Do not ask, "Can they answer it?" Ask, "What will each answer tell me?"

### Act 3: Hinge Questions Change the Lesson Path

Relevant slides: 14-17

Key message:

> A hinge question is a decision point.

Narration:

"A hinge question is based on the concept students must understand before the lesson can move forward. Every student answers. The teacher interprets responses quickly. The answer pattern determines the next move."

Use this as a simple implementation routine for teachers:

1. Pick one critical concept.
2. Write one diagnostic question.
3. Collect all responses.
4. Decide the next instructional step.

## Suggested Opening Script

"This session is not about asking more questions. It is about asking questions that reveal the thinking we need to see."

## Suggested Closing Script

"If a question does not change what the teacher does next, it may still be useful for practice or discussion, but it is not doing the full formative assessment job."

## Workshop Activity

Ask every teacher to rewrite one existing question into a diagnostic question. The revised question must include:

- one correct answer;
- at least three plausible wrong answers;
- a note explaining what each wrong answer reveals;
- a planned response for the most common wrong answer.

The final message:

> Better questioning is not more teacher talk. It is better evidence of student thinking.
