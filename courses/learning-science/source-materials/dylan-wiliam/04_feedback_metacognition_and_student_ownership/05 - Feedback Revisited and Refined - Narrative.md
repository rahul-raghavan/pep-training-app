# Narrative for "Feedback Revisited and Refined"

## Core Thesis

Feedback works only when the learner is ready, the culture is trustworthy, and the task actually benefits from feedback.

This is a refinement deck. It asks a more nuanced question than "How do we give better feedback?" It asks when feedback is appropriate, how culture changes feedback reception, and how feedback connects to motivation, mindset, and recording/reporting.

## The Story in One Paragraph

The deck starts by asking when feedback is appropriate and distinguishes different kinds of knowledge and learning. It then focuses on the culture needed for feedback: students must interpret critique as help toward high standards rather than as judgement of fixed ability. The wise feedback studies are central: critical comments are more likely to be used when students believe the teacher has high expectations and confidence in them. The deck also treats growth mindset carefully, showing that mindset effects depend on school norms and challenge-seeking culture.

## The Four Acts of the Deck

### Act 1: Not Every Learning Situation Needs the Same Feedback

Relevant slides: 3-9

Key message:

> Optimal instruction depends on what is being learned.

Narration:

"Wiliam begins by distinguishing biologically primary and biologically secondary knowledge. Some things are learned through guided play, exploration, or authentic activity. Others require explicit teaching and structured feedback. The nature of the learning should shape the nature of feedback."

Practical caution:

> Do not make feedback a ritual. Use it where it can change learning.

### Act 2: Feedback Requires Trust

Relevant slides: 10-20

Key message:

> Students need to believe feedback is a sign of high standards and belief, not rejection.

Narration:

"The wise feedback studies are the emotional center of this deck. Critical feedback works better when paired with a message that the teacher has high expectations and believes the student can meet them. Students are more likely to revise when feedback is interpreted as investment."

Teacher-facing phrase:

> "I am giving you this feedback because the work matters and I believe you can improve it."

### Act 3: Mindset Works Through Norms and Choices

Relevant slides: 21-25

Key message:

> Growth mindset is useful when it changes challenge-seeking and strategy use.

Narration:

"Wiliam avoids simplistic mindset language. Mindset interventions are not magic. They have stronger effects in environments where challenge-seeking is supported. The goal is not for students to say they have a growth mindset. The goal is for them to choose harder work, use better strategies, and persist productively."

### Act 4: Recording and Reporting Should Not Undermine Learning

Relevant later slides

Key message:

> If reporting systems turn feedback into judgement too quickly, they can weaken learning.

Narration:

"The deck's reporting theme matters for school systems. If every piece of work is converted into a grade, students may stop seeing work as improvable. Recording should support learning conversations, not replace them."

## Suggested Opening Script

"This session is about feedback after we have already accepted that feedback can help. The better question is: under what conditions will students actually use it?"

## Suggested Closing Script

"The best feedback culture combines high standards, trust, time to improve, and routines for revision. Without those conditions, even technically accurate feedback can fail."

## Workshop Activity

Teachers rewrite one piece of critical feedback with a wise-feedback frame:

1. State the high standard.
2. Communicate belief that the student can meet it.
3. Give a specific next action.
4. Provide time for revision.

The final message:

> Feedback is received through relationship, culture, and identity, not just cognition.
